# AgriN Three-Minute Pitch

## 0:00 — Problem
Farmers and agricultural officers often face fragmented signals: weather, crop condition, soil records, and local field observations are scattered, while climate risk moves faster than manual reporting.

## 0:20 — AgriN solution
AgriN brings farm context and risk signals into one decision workflow. This prototype runs without API keys and marks seeded data clearly, so the core experience can be demonstrated offline.

## 0:40 — Farmer command center
Show the sample cotton farm in Nanded. The dashboard combines a deterministic health score, a local weather fixture, crop stage, missing soil test data, and early warnings. Every proposed action includes a reason and confidence context.

## 1:10 — Crop intelligence
Open the advisor and crop planner. The recommendation engine uses transparent weights and farm context instead of returning an unexplained chatbot answer. The current rules are a prototype, not a validated yield model.

## 1:30 — Disease and field signals
Show the symptom-guided triage and explain its boundary: the selected photo stays in the browser, and the current result is based on farmer-selected symptoms, not computer vision. The map and vegetation values are explicitly labeled demonstration layers.

## 1:50 — Regenerative agriculture
Show the practice transitions and 90-day sequence. Benefits are described qualitatively, not as invented soil-carbon or yield measurements.

## 2:10 — Officer operations
Show illustrative district alert priorities and the resource redistribution example. Stress that the counts and inventory signals are not connected to official systems.

## 2:30 — BRICS cooperation
Show the federated-learning concept: national data can remain local while approved model updates or aggregated signals are shared. This is a simulation, not a live multi-country network.

## 2:50 — Impact and next step
AgriN’s difference from a chatbot is the structured farm context, deterministic transparent rules, alerts, map, activity history, and role-oriented views. The next investment is validation with agronomists and farmers, verified provider feeds, secure identity, and an audited data-governance design.
